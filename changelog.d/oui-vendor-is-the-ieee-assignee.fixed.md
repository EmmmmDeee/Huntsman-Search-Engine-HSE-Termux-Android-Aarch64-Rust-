- `network BSSID` now gives the manufacturer the IEEE OUI registry actually names, as its evidence says it does. A hand-curated table took precedence over the registry, and 36 of its 110 rows named a different company: `f4:9f:54` was "Apple" where the registry says Samsung, `00:c0:4f` "Sony" where it says Dell, and `d4:61:da` "Google Pixel" where it says Apple. Another 21 rows were not assigned at all. The vendor is now always the registry's assignee (`3c:5a:b4` reads "Google, Inc."). The table keeps a device class only for the 57 prefixes the registry assigns to the brand the row expects, and a test holds every row to the registry.
- A BSSID whose prefix no manufacturer is registered for, or that sits in a block the IEEE Registration Authority sub-assigns, now gives the `oui` row as a validated zero with no vendor. It used to report success with the manufacturer "Unknown" or "IEEE Registration Authority".
- `oui::classify_mac`, `is_locally_administered` and `is_multicast` accept only a MAC written as 12 hex digits, bare or separated by `:`, `-` or `.`. Text that merely contains hex letters, such as "not-a-mac" or "Cafe Babe", is no longer read as a randomised address. `OuiInfo::manufacturer` gives the registry's manufacturer, or none.
