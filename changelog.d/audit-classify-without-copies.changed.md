- Classifying a response no longer copies its body. `fetch::finish` copied every body into a `String` to classify it and dropped the copy at once while the caller read the original bytes; the status-blind classifier that crt.sh and DNS over HTTPS use lowercased the whole body and ran its substring scans before it looked at the first byte, although a JSON body, which is what those endpoints answer, decides the verdict on its own. The classifiers now read the body through `Response::text_view`, a borrowed view that copies only a body that is not valid UTF-8, and `classify::is_challenge` checks the JSON opener first. Verdicts are unchanged. On a 3.7 MB crt.sh-shaped answer the status-blind classification drops from 12.9 ms to under a microsecond (`examples/classify_bench.rs`, median of seven, release build on x86_64); a unit test holds that `text_view` borrows a UTF-8 body.
