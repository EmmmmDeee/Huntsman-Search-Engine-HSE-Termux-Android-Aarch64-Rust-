- `scan`, the web lookup and `scan --recurse` accept a phone number written with the ITU `00` prefix, as `phone` already did. `scan "0044 20 7183 8750"` was routed to the phone lookup by one phone rule and then refused (exit 65, "scan selector is not canonical") by another. Both now use the same rule.
- An Australian number written with its trunk zero after the country code, such as `+61 (0)412 345 678` or `0061 (0)412 345 678`, is `+61412345678`, the same number the rest of Huntsman names. `phone` used to print and save it as `+610412345678`.
- A number followed by an extension, such as `x12`, `ext. 12`, `;ext=12`, `#12` or a `,,` pause, is refused. Huntsman used to drop the marker and join the extension onto the number, so `+1 415 555 2671 x12` became the different number `+1415555267112`, reported at high confidence. A vanity number with letters between its digits is refused for the same reason. Words after the number, such as `(office)`, are still accepted.
- The dialling-code table names South Sudan (+211), Diego Garcia (+246), Jamaica's +1 658, Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba (+599 7, 3 and 4), and Kazakhstan (+7 6 and +7 7). Before, +211 numbers failed, +1 658 was the US, +599 3, 4 and 7 were Curaçao, and Kazakhstan's numbers were Russia, under the mistaken label "Russia / Kazakhstan (NANP)". +7 is now just "Russia".
